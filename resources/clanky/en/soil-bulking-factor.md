## Soil bulking factor for common soils

| Soil | Bulking factor |
|:---|---:|
| Sand and gravel | 1.15 |
| Topsoil | 1.20 |
| Ordinary soil (loam) | 1.25 |
| Clay | 1.30 |
| Broken rock | 1.45 |

Multiply the volume in the ground by the factor and you get the volume on the heap or on the lorry. The values are approximate and apply straight after digging. The real figure depends mainly on the type of soil and how compact it was.

## Why there are two volumes

**Bank volume** is what was in the ground. It tells you how much is dug out and how much goes back as backfill, which is compacted in layers.

**Loose volume** is what lies on the heap and on the lorry. You use it to plan space on the plot and the [muck away](clanek:excavation-calculator). Prices can use either: earthworks may be quoted in bank cubic metres, haulage per load or per hour, and tipping usually per tonne. So ask which cubic metres a price means.

## How much it weighs

Roughly 1.5 to 1.9 t per m³ in the ground, ordinary soil around 1.8 t, wet soil more. Bulking changes the volume, not the weight. 10 m³ of ordinary soil from the ground is 12.5 m³ on the lorry and weighs about 18 t. When you order a lorry, check what runs out first: the volume of the body or the payload. A UK grab lorry, for example, takes up to 16 tonnes, about 10 m³.

## How much soil you will move

How much your own house digs out and leaves over is worked out by the [excavation calculator](clanek:excavation-calculator), where you set the bulking factor in Details. It needs the length and width of the house and the length of the internal load-bearing walls, which you get when you [draw the floor plan in TupTuDu](aplikace:).
