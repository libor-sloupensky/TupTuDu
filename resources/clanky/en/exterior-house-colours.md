## Start with the roof and windows

Roofing and windows last for decades, a facade gets repainted more often. So choose it to suit them.

- **With a red clay tile roof**, calm warm tones work: cream, sand, ochre or warm white.
- **With a black or anthracite roof**, almost anything goes, most often white or light grey. A bolder colour works too, but on an insulated wall check its light reflectance value first.
- **White windows** go with any facade. **Anthracite windows** take bolder colours too, but stand out most on a light one. **Brown woodgrain windows** suit cream, sand, ochre and white.

Three colours are enough: facade, roof and windows. Give the front door and garage door the window colour, or make the front door the one bold accent. If you haven't chosen the roof yet, compare [types of roofs](clanek:types-of-roofs) on the same house.

## Neighbours and local rules

Look at the houses next door and at where your house is mainly seen from. Planning conditions, a design code or a homeowners' association can set colours and materials. In England, painting a house usually needs no planning permission, but a listed building may need listed building consent and in some conservation areas a change of colour needs permission. Ask the council before you order.

## Why a dark colour doesn't belong on external insulation

In full sun a dark facade can reach 70 °C or more, a light one around 40 °C. The insulation behind the render can't carry the heat away, so the render expands and shrinks more and can crack, especially at the corners of windows and of the house.

The lightness of a shade is its light reflectance value (LRV, in Central Europe HBW): 0 is black, 100 white. Makers of external wall insulation systems (EWI or ETICS, EIFS in North America) usually ask for an LRV of at least 20 to 25, some finishes more. The exact limit is in the render's data sheet. It isn't a law but a condition of the system, so agree a darker shade in advance. Darker colours are allowed on smaller, bounded areas, but the joint between light and dark cracks most.

## Why the sample misleads you

A small square in the shop looks different from a whole wall outside. The shade changes with the render's texture, the sun and the colour of the roof and the neighbours. Have the shade applied to a larger board and look at it outdoors in the morning and evening. Order render for the whole house at once, batches differ in shade.

## Try it on your own house

In TupTuDu you [draw the floor plan](clanek:how-to-draw-a-floor-plan), add a roof and windows and [colour them in 3D](aplikace:): the facade, the roof, windows separately outside and inside, doors and garage doors. For the facade, windows and doors you enter a shade from the maker's chart as a #rrggbb code or pick the nearest RAL. Tiles and slate come as looks based on photos, metal in any colour. Turn the house to see the sunny and shady side and send the result to your family by link.

Screen colour depends on the monitor and the RAL conversion is approximate, so use 3D to compare combinations and check the final shade on a sample. The facade gets one shade; cladding or a second colour on part of the house can't be set yet.
