## Minimum room sizes: England and the US as examples

| Room | England, space standard | US, residential code | What really fits |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Double bedroom | 11.5 m², the first at least 2.75 m wide | 70 sq ft (6.5 m²), at least 7 ft (2.13 m) each way, plus an emergency escape window | 3 × 3 m: 160 × 200 cm bed and two bedside tables, no wardrobe. 3 × 3.6 to 3.7 m: plus a wardrobe and a walkway. 3 × 4 m: comfortable |
| Single bedroom | 7.5 m², at least 2.15 m wide | as above | 2.6 × 3.1 m: 90 × 200 cm bed, 100 cm wardrobe, 120 cm desk and about 1 × 1.5 m free |
| Living room | not set | as above | a 220 cm sofa, coffee table and TV wall need a width of at least 3.3 m |
| Hallway | 90 cm on the entrance storey of a new home, more where you turn into a narrow door | 3 ft (91 cm) | 90 cm: one person. 120 cm: two people pass. 150 cm: they pass even next to a shoe cabinet |

England's building regulations set no general room size. The space standard applies only where the council has adopted it, the hallway rule comes from Approved Document M. US states build on the International Residential Code, often with their own changes. The right-hand column is our calculation with common furniture, not a rule.

## Minimum bedroom size for a 160 × 200 cm bed

A 160 × 200 cm bed, the European king size, takes about 165 × 210 cm with its frame. Leave at least 60 cm around it so both sides can be reached. A wardrobe is 60 cm deep, with sliding doors about 10 cm more, and needs 80 to 100 cm in front of it, depending on how the doors open.

A square 3 × 3 m bedroom, 9 m², leaves 90 cm at the foot of the bed. That is not enough for a wardrobe and a walkway, unless the bed goes against the wall and one of you climbs over the other. A wardrobe fits from about 11 m²: 3 × 3.6 m with sliding doors (210 + 70 + 80 cm), 3 × 3.7 m with hinged ones (210 + 60 + 100 cm). The position of the door matters too. A 3 × 4 m bedroom leaves almost 70 cm at the sides of the bed and 1.3 m between the bed and the wardrobe.

## Children's room and single bedroom

About 8 m² is enough for one child, but the shape matters: in a room 2.2 m wide, only 1.2 m is left beside the bed for a desk, wardrobe and walkway. For two children with two beds, plan on 12 m² or more.

## Hallway width: when two people can pass

In 90 cm one person walks and the other steps aside. From 120 cm two people pass, with a 30 cm shoe cabinet plan on 150 cm. A long sofa has to turn from the hallway into a door, so the narrower the door, the wider the hallway. England's rules for new homes measure the clear opening, about 50 mm less than the door leaf: with a 750 mm clear opening approached from the side the corridor must be 1,200 mm, with 800 mm 900 mm will do. A standard 826 mm leaf counts as 775 mm, which needs 1,050 mm. Stairs have their own rules, see the [stair calculator](clanek:stair-calculator).

## Ceiling height

England's space standard asks for 2.3 m from floor to ceiling over at least 75% of the home, London for 2.5 m. The US code asks for 7 ft (2.13 m) in habitable rooms and hallways. Under a sloping ceiling, floor area with less than 1.5 m of headroom doesn't count towards England's standard.
