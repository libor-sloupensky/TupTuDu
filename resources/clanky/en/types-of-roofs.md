## Types of roofs by shape

| Type | Shape | Common pitch | Loft | Keep in mind |
|:---|:---|---:|:---|:---|
| Gable | two planes, a ridge and two gable walls | 35–45° | large, windows in the gables | simplest structure, the gable walls get more rain |
| Hipped | planes on all four sides | 25–40° | smaller, cut back by the hips | dearer structure and flashings, the overhang shelters every wall |
| Mono-pitch (shed, skillion) | one plane, one eaves | 5–25° | only by the tall wall | all the water into one gutter, a tall wall on the high side |
| Flat | level with a slight fall | a few degrees | none | waterproofing and outlets need regular checks |

The pitches are common practice in Central Europe, not rules. In the UK, a roof up to 10° counts as flat. The roofing and your designer set the final pitch.

## Gable or hipped roof

A gable roof has a simple structure and a roomy loft, which windows in the gables light well. A steeper pitch gives more space under the roof, but also a higher ridge and more roofing.

A hipped roof slopes over the shorter sides of the house too. It looks lower and calmer and its overhang shelters every wall. The loft is smaller, though, and the hips add to the cost of the structure and the roofing. When the hips meet at one point on a square plan, it is a pyramid roof.

## Mono-pitch roof: who it suits and at what pitch

A mono-pitch roof is a single plane rising from the eaves to a tall wall. It suits modern houses, bungalows, extensions and garages. The tall wall often faces the sun, south in the northern hemisphere, to take large windows. The roof then faces away from the sun; for solar panels, the other way round works better.

The pitch is usually 5 to 25° and follows the roofing. Every covering has a minimum pitch set by its maker: metal and membranes go down to a few degrees, tiles need more, from about 12.5 to 15° for some interlocking concrete tiles to 35° for plain tiles (UK manufacturer data). At a low pitch, snow lies longer and all the water runs into one gutter, which must be big enough.

## Roof pitch and how to calculate it

Pitch is the angle between the roof plane and the horizontal. Take the ridge height above the point where the roof meets the outside face of the wall and the horizontal distance from there to the ridge: tan α = height / distance.

Example: a gable roof on a house 10 m wide has 5 m to the ridge. At 38°, the ridge is 5 × tan 38° ≈ 3.9 m above the wall face.

Pitch is also given as a ratio. In the US it is inches of rise per 12 inches: 3.9 m over 5 m is about 9.4 in 12, and 45° is 12 in 12. Elsewhere it is a percentage: here 78%, 45° is 100% and 30° only 58%. Convert a maker's minimum pitch before you compare it with degrees.

## Types of roofs on your own house in 3D

When you [draw the floor plan](clanek:how-to-draw-a-floor-plan) in [TupTuDu](aplikace:), the roof is created over the outline of the top storey. In the Storeys and areas panel you switch the type, pitch, overhangs, eaves height and roofing and see the result in 3D straight away, including the ridge height. The editor works in metric units. For an attic storey, the editor estimates the floor area above 1.2 m of headroom, the Czech threshold; England's space standard counts from 1.5 m.

Clicking an edge in the floor plan switches it between eaves and gable, so on a mono-pitch roof you choose which wall gets the eaves. Half-hipped, mansard and gambrel roofs have no preset in the editor. Try the [exterior colours](clanek:exterior-house-colours) along with the roof.
