## Before you start drawing

Have the plot dimensions and compass directions ready, and find out how far from the boundary you may build; the rules differ a lot between countries.

List the rooms with rough areas and split them into a day zone and a night zone. In the northern hemisphere, the living room goes south or west, bedrooms east and service rooms north; in the southern hemisphere, swap north and south. Keep bathrooms and the kitchen above and next to each other, they share pipes. How much space a bedroom or hallway needs is covered in [minimum bedroom size](clanek:minimum-bedroom-size).

## How to draw a floor plan step by step

1. **External walls.** Draw the outside outline at the real wall thickness, insulation included.
2. **Internal load-bearing walls.** With the external walls they carry the floor above, so place load-bearing walls upstairs on top of those below.
3. **Stairs.** In a two-storey house, place them before the partitions. The floor above needs an opening long enough for the headroom, 2 m in England for example, and free space before the first and after the last step. The [stair calculator](clanek:stair-calculator) works out the steps.
4. **Partitions.** They divide rooms, carry no floor and are thinner.
5. **Windows and doors.** Place windows by orientation, with the furniture under them in mind. The opening in the wall is wider than the door because of the frame. Keep doors out of corners and watch which way they swing.
6. **Rooms.** Name each enclosed space and compare its area with your list.
7. **Furniture.** Put in the bed, table and wardrobes at real size. Only now will you see whether the bedroom is big enough.
8. **Check.** Walk from the entrance to the kitchen and the bathroom: neither route should lead through another room. Check the outside dimensions and the distances to the boundaries.

| Element | Typical size or rule |
|:---|:---|
| External wall, insulated masonry, Central Europe | 40 to 55 cm |
| External cavity wall, new house in the UK | about 30 to 40 cm |
| External wall, timber frame, Central Europe | about 26 to 35 cm |
| External wall, 2×6 timber frame, US | about 19 to 23 cm |
| Partition | 10 to 15 cm |
| Internal door | 70 to 90 cm, UK standard leaf 76 cm (2 ft 6 in). On the entrance storey of a new home in England, doors to rooms and the WC need a 750 mm clear opening, in practice an 826 or 838 mm leaf |
| WC door on the entrance storey of a new home in England | opens outwards |
| Free space around a bed | at least 60 cm |
| Straight stairs to the upper floor | about 0.9–1 × 3 to 5 m, depending on storey height and how steep your rules allow |

:::pozor A common mistake: thin walls
If the real external wall is 50 cm and you draw 30 cm with the same outside dimensions, every room along the facade comes out 20 cm deeper than it will be. Across the house 40 cm is missing, and furniture that fitted on paper won't fit in the house.
:::
